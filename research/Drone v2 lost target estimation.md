# Teach the ghost to stop, turn and hide

*Handoff guide for the v2 work session on Evan's follow-me drone (Unity + Python sim). Research date 2026-11-17; current system is v1 (benchmark score 92.7).*

The v1 ghost target runs away because `TargetWorldTrack.predict()` is a single constant-velocity point: it keeps moving at the speed the person had when they vanished for up to 8 s, then freezes, while the re-lock gate grows at only 0.5 m/s. Real walkers do something else. They walk at about **1.3 ± 0.26 m/s**, can **stop within 0.6–0.9 s**, keep **80–100% of their speed round a 90° corner**, and the standard pedestrian filter assumes a turn or stop roughly every **6.7 s** of straight walking. So after 5–15 s behind an obstacle, "stopped just behind it", "turned along it" and "went into a door" are all physically likely, and a single extrapolated point is almost useless beyond about 10 s. v2 should replace the single point with a **map-constrained, multi-mode particle filter** (modes walk / turn / stop / entered-building). The filter should use **negative information**: any place the cameras can clearly see but where the person is not detected loses probability. The drone should then fly to the **viewpoint that sees the most remaining probability**, instead of orbiting a guessed spot. Re-lock should use belief density × appearance instead of a fixed circle. This design matches the closest research systems (an occlusion-aware Bernoulli particle filter, a 2025 UAV occlusion-aware search planner, and the RPF-Search person-following robot). No commercial follow drone does it: Skydio and DJI coast for about 5 s and then rely on re-identification. Through-wall sensors exist, but nothing that fits a few-hundred-gram drone sees through concrete. For the real drone, a cooperative tag (UWB or phone GPS) is the reliable fallback, with 60 GHz radar and thermal as experiments. The first thing to build is not the filter but the benchmark: walls, buildings and doors in the mock world, a ghost-error metric, and five new occlusion scenarios. Without them, v2 cannot show what it fixed.

## Why the v1 ghost overshoots, in numbers from the v1 benchmark

The v1 results file (`benchmarks/v1.json`) shows where loss handling costs points. `pillar_occlusion` scores **81.6% on-target with a 5.9 s re-lock**. `decoy_at_exit` scores **68.6% on-target, 31.4% lost, 11.6 s re-lock, and 3.0 s closer than 2 m** to the target. Every other scenario is at 92% or higher. The decoy scenario is exactly Evan's complaint. The target walks east at 1.2 m/s, **stops** in the "shop" for 8 s (hidden from t = 6 to 14 s), then walks on. v1 extrapolates the ghost east at 1.2 m/s, so after 8 s it sits about 9.6 m past the real person. The gate radius is only 4 + 0.5 × 8 = 8 m. When the person reappears at the spot where they vanished, they are outside the gate. The drone has meanwhile flown to and orbited the wrong spot, where the look-alike decoy is walking (my arithmetic from the scenario definition in `core/tools/benchmark.py`). The appearance memory correctly refused the decoy (wrong 0%), so the cost shows up as lost time. The best possible re-lock in this scenario is about 8.5 s (8 s hidden plus 0.5 s confirmation). **v1 wastes about 3 s searching in the wrong place**, plus the too-close penalty.

Two code facts matter for the fix. The gate is a plug-in: `FollowTarget._world_gate()` returns a function `gate(track) -> metres`, which `TargetSelector.select()` compares with `reacquire_gate_m = 4.0`. A belief-based gate can therefore replace it without touching the selector. Second, the mock "shop" in `decoy_at_exit` is only a time window (`target_hidden=((6, 14),)`). The person becomes invisible while standing in open ground. The mock world has no walls, buildings or doors, only infinitely tall round pillars (`MockWorldConfig.obstacles`). That matters for v2. Negative information would correctly say "I can see that spot clearly and nobody is there", and push probability **away** from where the person really is. So the shop must become real geometry (a box the person is inside) before the new filter is scored on it. Also, `run_scenario()` only builds a `VoxelMap` for sonar scenarios. The particle filter needs an obstacle map in every scenario.

## What human movement research says the ghost should do

### Speed, stopping and turning set the physical limits

The social-force model draws desired walking speed from **N(1.34, 0.26) m/s**, caps speed at 1.3 × desired, and uses a **0.5 s relaxation time** to reach that speed ([Helbing & Molnár 1998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9805244)). Field data agree: 15th-percentile walking speed is 1.25 m/s for ages 14–64 and 0.97 m/s for 65+ ([Knoblauch et al. 1996](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/field-studies-of-pedestrian-walking-speed-and-start-up-time/)). A 2024 review puts the design value at 1.25 m/s and finds **outdoor speeds about 38% higher than indoor** ([Giannoulaki & Christoforou 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/11/4813/html)). Browsing behaviour is very different: people at a science fair moved at a modal **0.6 ± 0.3 m/s** ([Gutiérrez-Roig et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03604)). Joggers move at about 1.8–2.7 m/s ([Marathon Handbook, aggregator](https://marathonhandbook.com/average-running-speed/)).

Stopping is fast. In 20 young adults walking at 1.16 m/s, **a planned stop took 0.86 s and an unplanned stop 0.58 s** ([Appl. Sci. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/12/7323/html)). That works out to roughly 1.3–2.0 m/s² of deceleration and under a metre of travel. Corners barely slow people down. In a 90° turn around a corner, normal walkers went from 1.43 to **1.27 m/s (about 11% slower)**, and passed the corner with only **0.21–0.28 m** between their centre of mass and the corner ([CDC/NIOSH](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/30209/cdc_30209_DS1.pdf)). The practical meaning: within 1–2 s of vanishing, a person can be fully stopped or have turned hard round the obstacle's edge. Particles should hug corners, not swing wide.

### How often people stop, turn, or keep going

There is no study measuring where an occluded pedestrian reappears. The available proxies all point the same way. In the TrajNet++ real-world test set, **3.3% of 4.8 s trajectories are static and 22.7% are near-linear**; the rest interact or curve ([Kothari et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.03639)). A pedestrian IMM uses mean mode durations of **6.66 s straight walking, 2.5 s turning, 1.67 s manoeuvring** ([Schneider & Gavrila 2013](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/29423833/157078_gcpr13.pdf)). Stops at the science fair followed two time-scales, about **37.5 s short pauses** and about **10 min long stays** ([Gutiérrez-Roig et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03604)). From these, the research notes propose priors for the first 5–15 s of occlusion: **straight 60–80%, turn along the obstacle or path 15–30%, stop 5–15% (more near doors and benches), U-turn a few percent**. These are engineering guesses to tune, not measured values.

### Constant velocity is a good short guess and a bad long one

For short horizons, constant velocity is hard to beat. On ETH/UCY at 4.8 s, a constant-velocity model scored **ADE 0.39 m / FDE 0.83 m**, better than LSTM, RED and SR-LSTM. **Constant acceleration was far worse (0.84 / 2.11 m)**. History beyond the last step added nothing, and adding **25° heading noise** with best-of-20 scoring cut FDE to 0.56 m ([Schöller et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1903.07933)). Pedestrian filters that mix constant-velocity and constant-turn models beat either alone, by up to 30 cm during manoeuvres ([Schneider & Gavrila 2013](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/29423833/157078_gcpr13.pdf)). Longer horizons need the map and the person's likely goal. Y-Net, which first guesses goals and then paths to them, is what makes 30 s prediction workable on the Stanford Drone campus dataset ([Mangalam et al. 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Mangalam_From_Goals_Waypoints__Paths_to_Long_Term_Human_Trajectory_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf)). For a single person, scene and goal context matter more than modelling other people ([Schöller et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1903.07933)). Extrapolating the ETH/UCY numbers gives a rough error budget for a walker: about 0.1–0.2 m at 1 s, about 1 m at 5 s, and several metres by 10 s. After about 10 s, the drone should reason over a set of places, not one point.

## What search algorithms say the drone should do

### Particles beat a Gaussian once the belief wraps round obstacles

The closest published estimator is Lockheed Martin's urban tracker ([Berry & Bucci 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02397)). It is a Bernoulli particle filter with **5,000 particles**, and it uses the map in three ways. **Particles inside buildings get zero weight. Detection probability is set near zero in shadow regions**, so not seeing the target there is not evidence against it. **New "birth" particles appear only in line-of-sight regions**. With the map, it kept the track through five shadowed segments. Without it, it lost the track and re-acquired slowly. Its target was a 13 m/s vehicle, not a pedestrian. A Gaussian (EKF) belief cannot bend round a corner; Gao et al. say so directly, and their follow-up, ASPIRe, switched to weighted particles for that reason ([Gao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.06363); [ASPIRe 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.01674)). The basic Bayesian search update after an unsuccessful look is **p' = p(1−q)/(1−pq)**, where q is the detection probability; the other cells are renormalised upward ([Bayesian search theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_search_theory)).

An IMM filter alone does not survive long occlusions. With no measurements, its mode probabilities fall back to the Markov chain's steady state and its covariance grows without limit. Use the IMM idea to **give each particle a mode** instead. Two more ideas transfer directly. **RPF-Search** removes search candidates the person could not have reached: it compares geodesic (Dijkstra) distance over the map against elapsed time × **v_max = 1.5 m/s** ([Ye et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02188v1)). **Hague & Wolek** keep a separate "unobserved probability" layer. It is zeroed where the UAV has looked and then spreads again with the motion model, so the drone neither re-searches cleared ground nor forgets it ([arXiv 2511.07822](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07822)).

### Look where probability × detection chance is highest

Search theory's rule, going back to Koopman, is to look where probability times detection chance is highest, update after each miss, and repeat ([Bayesian search theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_search_theory)). Two recent systems apply it to occluded targets. **Hague & Wolek's** occlusion-aware UAV planner found a ground target in **11 s versus 24 s for a coverage tour (DTSPN) and 62 s for a lawnmower** in one example. Its median localisation time was lower in medium and dense maps. Its failure mode was a greedy pull toward the map centre ([arXiv 2511.07822](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07822)). **RPF-Search** scored frontier candidates on a belief field with a reachability gate. In simulation it reached **100% success after topographic occlusion versus at best 94% (as low as 65%) for greedy next-best-view baselines**, and on a real robot it succeeded **10/10 versus 2/10 and 3/10** ([Ye et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02188v1)). Its benchmark included U-turns and hidden rooms, which are the cases v1 gets wrong.

### Commercial drones coast and hope, so v2 can go further

Skydio does **"predictive reacquisition for approximately 5 seconds"** behind buildings, trees or vehicles, re-identifies in about 3–5 s if the subject keeps a roughly constant velocity, and can switch to a similar-looking person in that window ([Skydio support](https://support.skydio.com/hc/en-us/articles/45768944808347)). DJI documents no strategy ([Heliguy](https://www.heliguy.com/blogs/knowledge-base/what-improvements-does-activetrack-7-0-have-compared-with-the-previous-version/)). Research trackers fly to the last observation and then along the extrapolated path: Fast-Tracker ([arXiv 2011.03968](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.03968)), and Fast-Tracker 2.0, which adds a spinning gimbal ([arXiv 2103.06522](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.06522)). Eva-Tracker searches a 2.5 m arc round the predicted point, and its authors say this "only handles short losses" ([arXiv 2602.12549](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12549)). v1 already does about as well as these. Searching through a 10–60 s loss with a probabilistic belief would put v2 ahead of published follow drones, not just level with them.

### The budget fits in Python

A numpy micro-benchmark in the research notes (my own measurement in this environment, not a published figure) covered the full filter step: predict, wall reject/reflect, 32-sample line-of-sight ray-cast to every particle, negative update, and resample. It took **2.7 ms at 2,000 particles, 17 ms at 10,000, and 120 ms at 50,000**. The ray-cast dominates. Viewpoint scoring with 100 candidates × 500 subsampled particles × 32 samples is about 1.6 M voxel lookups, roughly 20–40 ms. It should therefore run at 2–5 Hz, not every tick. Published C++ trackers plan in 3–20 ms ([Elastic](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.07111); [Fast-Tracker 2.0](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.06522)). The v1 benchmark tick is about 22 ms including mock rendering, so **3,000 particles at the tick rate is affordable**.

## Through-wall sensing helps the real drone only a little

None of the "see through walls" options fits a small follow drone today, except a tag the person carries. Water and concrete defeat 60 GHz. At 73 GHz, a 195 mm cinder block costs about **37 dB one way**, against **1.9 dB for drywall and 2.6 dB for glass**; 11 mm plywood costs about 8 dB ([Hosseini et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12568)). Radar pays that loss twice (out and back). Under the R⁴ law, a block wall cuts range roughly tenfold (inference). Low-GHz systems do penetrate. **WiDeSee** detected walking people through a 40 cm concrete wall 96–98% of the time from a drone flying at 1 m/s. It managed about 13–15 m through walls, and in a building 90% of its errors were within 4.6 m, but it flew on a heavy DJI S1000 ([Chen et al. 2019](https://zwang4.github.io/publications/sensys19.pdf)). A hovering UAV with UWB impulse radar detected breathing at 0.4–5 m with 93–98% accuracy, but only after motion compensation ([Jing et al. 2022](https://0-www-mdpi-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/2504-446X/6/9/235)). TI says radar vital-sign sensing from a UAV needs **about 20 s of stillness within 6 m**, and that drone vibration in the heartbeat/breathing band is a problem ([TI E2E](https://e2e.ti.com/support/sensors-group/sensors/f/sensors-forum/1596591/iwr6843isk-selection-of-iwr6843-for-6m-vital-signs-detection-on-uav-platform)).

The recommendation for the real drone, later, is in this order. First, stream the user's **phone GPS** into the belief as a wide measurement (3–10 m accuracy is a typical figure, not verified here). Second, add a **UWB tag** for metre-level re-finding behind walls. Third, try **thermal** for night. Treat 60 GHz radar as a hover-only experiment for thin occluders such as tents, hedges and glass shopfronts. Use NCC-certified modules: Taiwan regulates low-power radio devices under LP0002, updated in 2020 and amended about 2024 ([IB-Lenhardt](https://ib-lenhardt.com/news/low-power-radio-frequency-devices-technical-regulations-updated); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/ko-kr/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-5-2024/taiwan-lp0002-technical-regulations-of-low-power-radio-frequency-devices-amendment)). Through-wall sensing of people has raised privacy concerns in the US ([Range-R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range-R)). In the sim, all of these can be modelled later as one extra measurement type: a noisy range and bearing that ignores occlusion.

## Recommended v2 architecture

v2 adds one new use case, `TargetBelief`, between the world track and the loss-handling states. It changes three things: what the ghost is, how re-lock is gated, and where the drone flies while the target is hidden.

**Belief.** The belief is a cloud of particles, each with position, velocity, mode and weight. While the target is visible, the existing `TargetWorldTrack` stays in charge and the belief stays idle. On loss (end of COASTING, 0.6 s), the belief is seeded from the track's position and velocity. Every tick it does five things. It **predicts** each particle with its mode's motion. It **clips** moves against a 2-D free-space mask sliced from the voxel map at person height (0.3–1.8 m), so particles cannot cross walls. It applies **negative information**: a particle loses weight if a camera could see it (inside a camera's view, within detection range, line of sight through known-free voxels) but no matching person was detected. It **injects** 1–3% "birth" particles over reachable, unobserved free space. It **resamples** when the effective particle count falls below N/2. Unknown voxels count as free for the person to walk through, but as blocked for line of sight. This means the drone never treats a place it has not mapped as "checked".

**Ghost and gate.** The ghost becomes the **heaviest cluster of particles**, not the mean. A cloud split between "behind the pillar" and "past the pillar" has its mean in the middle, where nobody is. Unity also shows the second and third clusters, faded by their weight. The re-lock gate becomes `belief density near the candidate × appearance similarity`. Re-lock needs a margin over the runner-up and the existing 0.5 s confirmation. A stopped person standing where they vanished therefore passes the gate even when the main cluster has moved on.

**Search.** The search replaces "fly to predicted point, then orbit" with **viewpoint selection**. Every 0.2–0.5 s, sample 50–100 candidate positions. Put them on rings of 4, 7 and 10 m around the top clusters, beside occluder edges ("peek" points), and at a higher altitude, up to the existing 10 m line-of-sight cap. Score each candidate by `Σ weight × P_d(range) × line-of-sight`, using 500 particles predicted to the arrival time, divided by `(flight time + 2 s)`. Fly to the best one, and only switch when a new one scores at least 20% better (hysteresis). The phases follow the evidence. From 0 to about 3 s, follow the main cluster, as Skydio and Fast-Tracker do. From 3 to 20 s, use viewpoint selection. After 20 s, climb and cover the largest unobserved reachable mass. If most of the mass has gone into a building, hold a position that sees the door or doors. Give up when the probability the drone could still see from any reachable viewpoint stays below a threshold, rather than on a fixed timer.

## New benchmark scenarios and metrics

Each new scenario should run with **10 seeds**, where the seed picks the hidden behaviour (for example straight, left or right, or stop length). That prevents tuning to one script. Report each scenario's mean and its worst seed. All of them need item #1 (walls, boxes, doors).

| Scenario | Set-up | What it tests | Pass goal (proposed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stop_behind_pillar` | Walks 1.4 m/s behind a 5 m pillar, stops 10 s in its shadow, walks on | Evan's complaint: the ghost must not run away | Re-lock ≤ 1.5 s after the person is geometrically visible; ghost error at 10 s ≤ 2 m |
| `turn_at_corner` | Walks along an 8×8 m building wall, turns 90° round the corner (left/right by seed) | Map-constrained turning; corner-hugging | Truth inside top-3 clusters ≥ 90% of hidden time |
| `u_turn_hidden` | Goes behind a 10 m wall, U-turns after 3–6 s, comes back out on the entry side | Negative information plus a reversal mode | Re-find ≤ 3 s after exit; no flight to the far side for more than 5 s |
| `enters_building` | Walks into a door of a 10×6 m shop, stays 20 s, leaves by the same door or a second door (by seed) | ENTERED mode and door watching | Hovers with the door(s) in view; re-lock ≤ 2 s after exit |
| `long_occlusion_30s` | Walks behind a 40 m hedge/wall row for about 30 s and leaves at the end or through a gap (by seed) | Long horizon, climb, coverage | Re-find ≤ 5 s after exit; no collisions |
| `decoy_at_exit` (geometric) | Existing scenario rebuilt with a real shop box; same decoy | Negative information must not erase the real spot | wrong % = 0; relock_s ≤ 9.5 s |
| `decoy_turns` | Target turns at a corner while hidden; a same-shirt decoy walks straight on along the constant-velocity line | Belief gate plus appearance against a well-placed decoy | wrong % = 0 in all seeds |
| `junction_choice` | Hidden at a T or X junction between buildings; random exit | Goal hints (item #9) | Truth in top-3 ≥ 80% at 10 s |
| `slow_browser` | 0.6 m/s with 5–40 s pauses, two occlusions | Speed prior not fixed at 1.3 m/s | Ghost error at 10 s ≤ 2 m |
| `jogger_occlusion` | 2.5 m/s behind a row of 3 buildings | v_max switch to jogging | Re-find ≤ 3 s after exit |
| `never_returns` | Enters a building and stays for the whole 90 s | Safe give-up | Reaches GAVE_UP/RTH; never flies more than 15 m from the door; no collisions |

New metrics to add to `run_scenario()` and `REPORT.md` are the following. **Ghost error (m)** is the distance from the main cluster to the true position, as the mean over hidden time and as values at 5, 10 and 20 s. **Top-3 hit %** is the share of hidden time with the truth within 2 m of one of the three heaviest clusters. **Belief mass near truth** is the summed weight within 2 m, which shows whether the filter is confident and wrong. **Re-find delay (s)** runs from the first tick the target is geometrically visible to any camera to the confirmed re-lock. This separates "the person was hidden" from "the drone was slow", which `relock_s` mixes together. The bench also needs a count of **wrong re-locks**, the **search path length (m)**, and **time searching the wrong side** (the drone more than 10 m from the truth while the truth is visible from elsewhere). Keep `score()` as it is for the original 11 scenarios. Add a second "occlusion suite score" so v1's 92.7 stays comparable.

## Open questions for Evan

The guide assumed several answers that only Evan can give. Is the typical target walking, jogging or browsing? That sets v_max and the stop rate. Will the Unity scene get buildings with doors, or should the mock world be the only scoring authority for occlusion? Does a campus map exist for the real drone (OpenStreetMap footpaths and building outlines), or must the drone rely only on its own 30×30 m voxel map? Note that particles beyond 15 m fall off that map. How long should the drone wait at a door before flying home? Is it acceptable to ask the person to carry a phone or UWB tag, which changes what "lost" means? What computer will run the real drone? A Raspberry Pi-class board may need item #10 early. Should the score formula change once occlusion scenarios dominate, or stay as it is with a second column?

## Conclusion

The research turns "where did they go?" from a prediction problem into a **search problem with a memory of where the drone has already looked**. The single best gain is not a smarter motion model but negative information. The drone's four cameras and voxel map already know which places are visible and empty, and v1 throws that knowledge away. The motion research adds one simple correction, stopping, which explains Evan's observation and most of the lost time in `decoy_at_exit`. It also supplies the numbers that keep the particle cloud physically honest. What remains unknown is how real campus pedestrians split between continuing, turning and stopping behind an obstacle; no study measures it. The seeded scenarios therefore matter as much as the filter, because they stop v2 from being tuned to one scripted person. Hardware cannot shortcut any of this on a small drone, except a tag the person chooses to carry.
